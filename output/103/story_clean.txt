The incubus presses its obsidian weight into your sternum, a squat, simian parasite feasting on the rhythmic collapse of your paralyzed lungs. Henry Fuseli’s The Nightmare is not a dream, but the cold physics of suffocation rendered in oil. Observe the woman’s ivory gown cascading toward the floor, her arm dangling limp as a dead root while the creature’s clawed feet indent the soft flesh of her midsection. Behind the suffocating gloom, the pale, hollow eyes of the mare pierce the velvet curtain, a silent spectator to your inevitable expiration. This is the physiological architecture of terror, where the mind is a prisoner to its own anatomy and the demon is merely the physical manifestation of your body failing you. Sleep is not sanctuary; it is an invitation to the dark.
